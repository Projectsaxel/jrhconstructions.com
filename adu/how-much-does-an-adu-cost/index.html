How Much Does an ADU Cost?

The cost of building an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) varies depending on the type of structure, location, size, and level of finishes. Most homeowners can expect to invest between $80,000 and $350,000 or more.

Garage conversions are generally the most affordable option. Detached ADUs usually require the highest investment because they involve new foundations and utility connections.

Average ADU Costs by Type

Different types of ADUs have different construction costs. Existing structures often reduce the overall investment required.

Type of ADU Typical Cost Range
Garage Conversion ADU $80,000 to $180,000
Basement ADU $90,000 to $200,000
Attached ADU $120,000 to $250,000
Detached ADU $150,000 to $350,000+
Above Garage ADU $140,000 to $300,000

Factors That Affect ADU Costs

No two ADU projects are identical. Several factors can significantly influence the final budget.

Size of the ADU

Larger units require more materials and labor. Additional square footage also increases the cost of electrical, plumbing, and HVAC systems.

Type of Construction

Detached ADUs generally cost more than attached units or conversions. They require independent foundations and additional site work.

Site Conditions

Sloped lots, drainage issues, and difficult access can increase construction expenses. Some properties may also require additional excavation.

Interior Finishes

Premium cabinetry, countertops, flooring, and appliances can raise the overall investment. Homeowners should balance aesthetics with long-term value.

Utility Connections

Water, sewer, electrical, and HVAC connections represent a significant portion of the budget. Detached structures often require more extensive utility work.

ADUs also provide flexibility for multigenerational living and future housing needs. These benefits make them one of the most attractive residential improvements available today.

Financing Options for an ADU

Many homeowners do not pay for an ADU entirely out of pocket. Financing options can make these projects more accessible and easier to manage.

The best solution depends on the homeowner's equity, financial goals, and expected return on investment. Speaking with a lender can help determine the most suitable option.

Home Equity Loans

Home equity loans are one of the most common ways to finance an ADU. These loans allow homeowners to borrow against the value already built into their property.

Interest rates are often lower than personal loans. Fixed monthly payments also make budgeting easier.

HELOC

A Home Equity Line of Credit provides flexible access to funds. Homeowners can draw money as construction progresses.

This option works well for projects with multiple phases. Interest is typically paid only on the amount used.

Cash-Out Refinancing

Cash-out refinancing replaces an existing mortgage with a larger one. The difference between the old and new mortgage becomes available for the project.

This option may provide favorable interest rates. However, homeowners should carefully evaluate long-term costs.

Construction Loans

Construction loans are specifically designed for building projects. Funds are released in stages as work progresses.

These loans often convert into traditional mortgages after construction is complete. Requirements may vary depending on the lender.

Ways to Reduce ADU Construction Costs

Careful planning can significantly reduce expenses. Smart decisions during the design phase often have the biggest impact on the final budget.

Homeowners should focus on functionality and long-term value. Avoiding unnecessary complexity can lead to substantial savings.

Choose a Simple Floor Plan

Complex layouts increase labor and material costs. Simpler designs are generally more efficient and easier to build.

Open floor plans can maximize usable space. They also create a more modern appearance.

Use Standard Materials

Luxury finishes can quickly increase costs. Standard materials often provide excellent durability and aesthetics.

Homeowners can upgrade finishes in the future if desired. This approach helps control the initial investment.

Convert Existing Structures

Garage conversions and basement ADUs often cost less than detached structures. Existing walls and foundations reduce construction requirements.

This strategy can shorten project timelines as well. It is one of the most cost-effective ways to add living space.
